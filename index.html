01 / Arrival

Onodani valley, Nagano

You will be met at the road and after that nothing is asked of you.

Kaya is eleven rooms and three baths under one thatched roof, at the closed end of a valley that takes forty minutes to drive and about a day to stop hearing.

There is no key to your room. Dinner is at half past six because that is when it is ready. Beyond those two facts the house has no opinion about how you spend the time.

02 / The water

Yumori spring · 42.4°C at source

The water was here first.

The spring rises three hundred metres behind the house and reaches the baths in about eleven minutes. Long enough to lose two degrees, not long enough to lose anything else. It is drawn straight from the source, never reheated, never recirculated, and never topped up with anything but itself.

03 / The year

1,100 metres · three weeks behind the city

The blossom you arrived on lasts nine days.

Onodani sits at eleven hundred metres, so the valley runs about three weeks behind the city and everything here happens later than the calendar says it should. What follows is when things actually happen, which is the only version worth booking against.

  1. 春

    Spring

    Late April to mid-May

    The blossom opens around the twenty-eighth and is gone by the eighth. Nine days, most years. The water is warm and the air is not, which is the whole reason to be in it.

  2. 夏

    Summer

    June to August

    The river runs loud enough to sleep through and the nights stay under twenty degrees. The one season in which the cold plunge makes obvious sense.

  3. 秋

    Autumn

    Mid-October to November

    Maple the whole length of the east ridge. The busiest four weeks we have, and the only ones we ask you to book well ahead for.

  4. 冬

    Winter

    December to March

    Snow to the eaves and the rotenburo steaming up through it. The road is cleared by seven each morning. Eleven rooms, and most nights fewer than eleven guests.

Rooms open twelve months ahead. The autumn weeks go first.

04 / The rooms

Eleven, none of them the same

No architect drew these rooms. The house did.

Eleven rooms in a building that has grown in every direction since 1904. One has its own cedar tub off the back, one is dark on purpose, one takes the whole ridge through a wall of shoji. There are no tiers here and nothing to upgrade to. Tell us what you would like to wake up to and we will put you in the right one.

05 / A day at Kaya

Five hours that matter

15:00 17:30 18:30 21:00 06:00

Tea and a hot towel in the entrance hall. Someone takes your bag, and you do not see it again until it is already in your room.

The rotenburo is best now, while the valley still has some colour left in it and the stone is warmer than the air.

One seating, one menu. Whatever the river and the hillside gave up this week, cooked by people who saw it arrive.

Lights go down along the corridor. The house starts making its own noises, and none of them are yours to deal with.

The water is hottest before anyone else is awake. Take the ladle, take the far end, and watch the mist come off the ridge.
